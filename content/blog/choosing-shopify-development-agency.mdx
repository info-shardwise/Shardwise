A Shopify development agency should help you turn catalogue, customer, and operational requirements into a working store. Compare providers by how they scope the theme, configure integrations, test purchase journeys, and hand over access and maintenance responsibilities. A polished product page is useful, but the order must also reach the systems and people that handle it.

This guide helps UK and US merchants evaluate delivery proposals for a new store, a migration, or an existing-store update. Explore our [Shopify development service](/services/shopify-development/) for a discussion of your catalogue, storefront, integrations, and release scope.

## Start with how your store sells and fulfils orders

Describe your products, customers, markets, and the journey from product discovery to fulfilment. Include variants, stock availability, delivery options, and any systems used to manage orders. Identify unusual requirements early, such as subscriptions or product customisation.

For a fictional homeware business, the first release might need collections, products with colour variants, a supported payment setup, and a connection to an order-management tool. The brief should define which system owns stock information and what happens after a paid order. This is a planning example, not a Shardwise client case study.

Separate launch essentials from later ideas. A delivery team should explain the dependencies behind its estimate rather than applying the same timeline to every store.

## Prepare the catalogue before comparing layouts

Ask who prepares product titles, descriptions, images, options, and categories. Review representative products with realistic data so the design accounts for long names, missing optional information, and different image shapes.

Decide how customers browse and narrow their choices. Navigation, collection pages, search, and filtering should reflect the actual catalogue. A small catalogue and a large range with complex variants need different planning.

For a migration, inventory existing content and important URLs. Agree what is transferred, cleaned up, or left behind, and how the results will be checked. Our [website migration SEO checklist](/blogs/website-migration-seo-checklist/) provides broader questions about content, URLs, and release verification.

## Clarify theme configuration versus custom development

Ask whether the proposal configures a supported theme, extends one, or creates a tailored theme. Confirm which page types and states are included: product details, collections, cart, search, navigation, empty states, and content pages.

Shopify's [theme architecture documentation](https://shopify.dev/docs/storefronts/themes/architecture) explains templates, sections, blocks, and other parts of a theme. Use that context to ask which settings merchants can edit and which changes require a developer.

Request a demonstration of updating a product page or reusable section. Review the narrow-screen result and the experience with representative content. A custom theme should have a clear reason tied to the store, rather than being treated as an automatic requirement.

## Review apps and integrations as part of the scope

Inventory required apps and external services. For each, record the purpose, recurring cost, data flow, permissions, and support owner. A feature may be delivered by platform configuration, an app, custom code, or a combination; the proposal should explain which.

Ask which system owns inventory, product information, and order status. Describe what happens when an integration fails or sends an unexpected result. A working storefront can still have operational problems if orders or stock changes do not reach the next system.

Review app behavior on actual pages and purchase journeys. Confirm who tests compatibility after a theme or app update. Do not assume that installing an app completes every requirement associated with it.

## Check relevant delivery evidence

Ask to see work with similar catalogue or integration requirements. A small visual showcase does not demonstrate experience with a more complex trading operation. Request an explanation of the agency's actual role and the important decisions it made.

Where appropriate, review a permitted demonstration of product editing, an order flow, or the handover process. Respect confidentiality. Useful evidence can explain responsibilities and constraints without exposing customer records or private systems.

Our [agency selection guide](/blogs/how-to-choose-web-and-app-development-agency/) adds general questions about milestones, scope changes, and support. Keep the Shopify evaluation grounded in catalogue and trading tasks.

## Agree checkout and order testing before launch

Test the journey from finding a product through selecting options, adding it to the cart, reviewing totals, and completing the appropriate test checkout. Inspect the resulting order and confirm that notifications and connected systems behave as intended.

Shopify's [test-order guidance](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/checkout-settings/test-orders) describes verifying checkout and settings such as inventory, shipping, and notifications. Choose the supported testing approach for the store and payment provider. Payment test mode can prevent real purchases, so testing arrangements and restoration of live settings need explicit responsibility.

Agree the scenarios that matter to your customers: unavailable variants, changed quantities, invalid information, a supported discount, and a failed payment where the test method supports it. Shipping and tax settings should be approved by the merchant with appropriate advice, rather than inferred from a theme demonstration.

Review mobile layouts, keyboard access, and clear feedback. Our [accessibility checklist](/blogs/website-accessibility-checklist-small-business/) provides starting questions. Confirm the project's evaluation scope instead of relying on a single automated score.

## Use appropriate access and retain store control

Clarify how the agency will work on the store and which permissions it needs. Shopify's [collaborator account guidance](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/your-account/users/security/collaborator-accounts) describes a way for partners to request access with permissions controlled by the merchant.

Keep store ownership, billing responsibilities, and agency access clear. Review access at handover and when the engagement changes. Do not share the owner's password as a substitute for an agreed access process.

For a new store, ask which setup and transfer process applies. The [Shopify store-type documentation](https://shopify.dev/docs/apps/build/stores) distinguishes development, client-transfer, and merchant-store uses. Confirm the current workflow and plan requirements with the provider rather than assuming every test store can be handed over in the same way.

## Verify search, measurement, and release output

Agree responsibility for useful product content, titles, descriptions, internal links, redirects, and measurement configuration. Confirm who checks important old URLs after a migration. Our [technical and on-page SEO service](/services/technical-onpage-seo/) can support a scoped review.

Test key interactions after release, even when the preview passed. Inspect the published theme, app behavior, purchase settings, and incoming order records. Confirm that measurement reflects the intended customer actions before using it to judge results.

Performance depends on media, theme implementation, apps, and external scripts. Use the [Core Web Vitals checklist](/blogs/core-web-vitals-checklist-small-business/) to review representative pages. A new theme alone does not guarantee faster pages, more sales, or better rankings.

## Request handover and ongoing support details

Collect the theme source and agreed custom code, editing instructions, app inventory, subscription responsibilities, and release guidance. Ask for a walkthrough of common merchant tasks and the process for reporting an issue.

Confirm which defects are covered after launch and how new features are estimated. Define who reviews theme changes, app updates, catalogue errors, and purchase failures. Source files and account access are most useful when operational responsibilities are documented too.

## Questions for your first Shopify agency call

1. Which catalogue and operational requirements affect the estimate?
2. Why is the proposed theme approach appropriate?
3. Which apps and integrations are needed, and what do they cost to operate?
4. How will purchase and order-processing tests be demonstrated?
5. What content and URL migration is included?
6. Which accounts, files, and instructions are delivered at handover?
7. Who handles issues and updates after launch?

Record the answers against the same requirements for every shortlisted provider. Investigate unclear responsibilities before approving a delivery schedule.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do I need a custom Shopify theme?

Not always. A supported theme may meet the store's requirements with suitable configuration or extensions. Custom work is useful when it addresses specific design or interaction needs that justify development and maintenance.

### Will an agency upload all my products?

Only if product preparation and entry are included in the proposal. Confirm who supplies data, cleans it, imports it, and verifies the result.

### Does Shopify development include ongoing maintenance?

Only when agreed. Clarify initial support, recurring tasks, incident response, and the difference between fixes and new features.

### How much does a Shopify agency project cost?

Cost depends on catalogue complexity, theme work, apps, integrations, migration, testing, and support. Compare scoped estimates with recurring charges and exclusions rather than applying another store's price to your requirements.
